\section{Discussion}

NLDisco discovers interpretable discrete and continuous, environmental and behavioral features across timescales and levels of abstraction. Individual sparse latents map representations to source neural activity and provide hypotheses for further analysis and experiments. Feature representations recur across training runs and remain stable within a subject over time. A model trained on one session transfers its latents' interpretations to other sessions by fitting new input and output mappings, supporting the potential for a foundation model for interpretability. And in end-to-end comparisons of feature discovery, NLDisco matches the best methods on prespecified feature recovery and yields the highest share of interpretable latents as assessed by an AI agent judge.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, nosep, topsep=3pt]
    \item We provide NLDisco, an open-source pipeline for discovering interpretable neural representations.
    \item We introduce sparse encoder-decoders for temporally varying and nested features.
    \item We evaluate on synthetic and real neural data across species, brain regions, conditions, and scales.
    \item We benchmark NLDisco against other state-of-the-art LVMs, highlighting its advantages in discovering neural representations while showing it remains competitive in neural decoding and reconstruction.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, nosep, topsep=3pt]
    \item Latent interpretability remains manual and partly subjective.
    \item Learned latents reveal correlational structure, not causal mechanisms.  Causal claims require additional analyses with explicit assumptions.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Future work.}
Extending empirical validation to other neural recording modalities, such as neuroimaging, would highlight the generality of NLDisco. Evaluating CLTs and SCCs, which are implemented in the codebase but not shown here, would support cross-region prediction and ``brain diffing'': distinguishing shared and divergent features across regions, sessions, or subjects. Finally, real-time identification of neural representational features could enable closed-loop experiments in which latent activation triggers precisely timed stimulation or task changes: such experiments would connect feature discovery to causal implications. We are excited to share NLDisco with the community and look forward to future applications using this work.
