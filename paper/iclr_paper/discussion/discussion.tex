\section{Discussion}

NLDisco provides a practical pipeline for discovering interpretable features in neural population activity. Our results show that NLDisco can robustly discover discrete and continuous, environmental and behavioral feature representations across varying timescales and levels of abstraction. Importantly, same feature representations can be found across model training runs and are stable within a subject over time. This work highlights individual sparse latents as useful units of representational analysis, enabling a direct mapping from representation to source neural activity. The illustrated features are candidates for further analysis and experiments, highlighting NLDisco's role in hypothesis generation.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, nosep, topsep=3pt]
    \item We provide an open-source software package, NLDisco, that implements an end-to-end pipeline for discovery of interpretable neural representations.
    \item We introduce novel sparse encoder-decoder architectures that support discovery of temporally-varying and nested feature representations.
    \item We evaluate on both synthetic and real large-scale neural datasets, demonstrating utility across different species, brain regions, experimental conditions, and spatiotemporal scales.
    \item We benchmark NLDisco against popular state-of-the-art neural LVMs, CEBRA and LangevinFlow, demonstrating interpretable feature discovery while maintaining competitive neural decoding.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, nosep, topsep=3pt]
    \item Latent interpretability remains manual and partly subjective.
    \item Learned latents reveal correlational structure, not causal mechanisms.  Causal claims require additional analyses with explicit assumptions.
    \item Our targeted comparisons assess recovery of selected features rather than the breadth or effort of discovery across methods.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Future work.}
Extending empirical validation to other neural recording modalities, such as neuroimaging, would highlight the generality of NLDisco. Evaluating CLTs and SCCs, which are implemented in the codebase but not shown here, would support cross-region prediction and ``brain diffing'': distinguishing shared and divergent features across regions, sessions, or subjects. Finally, real-time identification of neural representational features could enable closed-loop experiments in which latent activation triggers precisely timed stimulation or task changes: such experiments would connect feature discovery to causal implications. We are excited to share NLDisco with the community and look forward to future applications using this work.
