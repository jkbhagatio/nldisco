\subsubsection{Sensitivity to the training batch size}
\label{subsubsection:rebuttal_batchsize}

BatchTopK keeps the $Bk$ largest activations across a training batch, so the activation threshold in each update depends on the batch size $B$; at inference, a fixed threshold calibrated on the training data is used. To test how the learned features depend on $B$, we retrained the Section~\ref{subsection:churchland_results} model ($D=192$, $k=16$, two Matryoshka levels) and the Section~\ref{subsection:aeon_results} model ($D=192$, $k=12$) at $B\in\{512,1{,}024,2{,}048\}$, where $1{,}024$ is the value used in the paper. The architecture, $k$, learning rate and warmup/cosine schedule are unchanged. The total number of training examples is also unchanged (10 epochs, 1{,}305{,}070 windows, for the macaque model; 5{,}120{,}000 windows for the Aeon model), so larger batches make proportionally fewer updates (2{,}550, 1{,}280 and 640 for the macaque model; 10{,}000, 5{,}000 and 2{,}500 for the Aeon model). We trained three seeds per batch size, none of them a seed of the paper models, so that the spread across the three $B=1{,}024$ models gives the scale of run-to-run variation.

We apply the selection rule of the corresponding analysis to every retrained model. For each feature description, among latents that are active ($z>0$) in at least 5\% of condition windows (and, for the macaque features, in at least 20 distinct condition trials), we take the highest-\metric{AUROC} latent and the highest-\metric{Sel} latent, scored on the same windows as in the paper (macaque: development windows; Aeon: all windows with complete wheel support). This rule recovers the paper's latents in the paper models (for the Aeon wheel feature, latent 52). To assess which latents of the paper model reappear, we correlate the activations of each of its 192 latents with those of every latent of the retrained model over the same windows; a latent reappears when its most correlated latent has correlation $r\geq0.5$, a threshold fixed before the retrained models were analysed. We also report the number of latents that never activate (dead latents) and the neural variance explained by the reconstruction (\autoref{table:rebuttal_batchsize}).

All four macaque features and the wheel feature are recovered at every batch size (\autoref{table:rebuttal_batchsize}). The best-matching latents' \metric{AUROC} and \metric{Sel} at $B=512$ and $B=2{,}048$ differ from their $B=1{,}024$ means by at most 0.033 \metric{AUROC} and 0.006 \metric{Sel} for the macaque features and by 0.018 \metric{AUROC} for the wheel feature; the seed-to-seed ranges of \metric{AUROC} at $B=1{,}024$ are 0.007--0.105. The best-\metric{Sel} latents reach $\metric{Sel}\geq0.974$ for all macaque features in every retrained model. On average 82--87\% of the paper model's latents reappear at each batch size, against 83--86\% (macaque) and 85--89\% (Aeon) for the individual $B=1{,}024$ seeds; the four other seeds of the paper's macaque model give 84--88\%. Every latent that the paper displays for the macaque features has a counterpart with $r\geq0.54$ at every batch size, and the counterpart of the wheel latent 52 has $r\geq0.82$. The macaque dictionaries have no dead latents, and the variance explained is 0.325 at $B=512$, 0.323 at $B=1{,}024$ and 0.317 at $B=2{,}048$. The Aeon dictionaries have on average 13.7 dead latents of 192 at $B=512$ (12--16), 2.7 at $B=1{,}024$ (1--6) and none at $B=2{,}048$; their variance explained is between 0.235 and 0.238 at all three batch sizes (0.240 for the paper model).
