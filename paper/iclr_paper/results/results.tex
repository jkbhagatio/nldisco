\section{Results}
\label{section:results}

We evaluate NLDisco on three open-source datasets:

\begin{enumerate}[topsep=1pt,itemsep=1pt,parsep=0pt]
    \item Simulated single-unit spike data of rat hippocampus during a navigation task (\hyperref[subsection:synthetic_results]{\ref*{subsection:synthetic_results}}).
    \item Unsorted spike data from macaque motor cortex during an active reaching task (\hyperref[subsection:churchland_results]{\ref*{subsection:churchland_results}}).
    \item Single-unit spike data from mouse retrosplenial cortex and superior colliculus during an ethological foraging assay (\hyperref[subsection:aeon_results]{\ref*{subsection:aeon_results}}).
\end{enumerate}

Together, these datasets span multiple species, brain regions, and experimental paradigms. We use the first to motivate the various NLDisco model architectures and test recovery of known ground-truth features, the second to assess performance on real neural data and benchmark against established neural LVMs, and the third to assess exploratory feature discovery and hypothesis generation in a naturalistic setting. Evaluation plots and metrics for NLDisco models in \hyperref[subsection:synthetic_results]{\ref*{subsection:synthetic_results}}, \hyperref[subsection:churchland_results]{\ref*{subsection:churchland_results}}, and \hyperref[subsection:aeon_results]{\ref*{subsection:aeon_results}} are shown in \hyperref[subsection:additional_results]{\ref*{subsection:additional_results}}. Throughout the results, $N$ denotes the number of input units, $S$ the number of timebins per input example, $D$ the total number of latents, $B$ the batch size, and $k$ the training batch top-$k$ sparsity parameter ($Bk$ latents are selected across a batch, not necessarily $k$ per example). For Matryoshka SEDs, $L$ denotes the number of nested levels, $D_\ell$ the number of available latents at level $\ell$ (with $D_L=D$), and $k_\ell$ its sparsity parameter. A single-level SED has $L=1$, $D_1=D$, and $k_1=k$.
