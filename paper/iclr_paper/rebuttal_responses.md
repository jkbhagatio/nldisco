## Reviewer 9Vz6 (4214 characters)

Thank you again for the detailed review and the follow-up. We have uploaded a revised manuscript. Your follow-up asked whether NLDisco can (1) train on multiple sessions without supervision, (2) give per-latent interpretations on them, and (3) generalize to a new recording session. Change 2 below does all three, and changes 4-7 and 11-13 answer your other points. ★ marks changes made in direct response to your review.

1. ★ **ST-NDT as a further baseline (Sec. 4.2, Fig. 4, Table 1, App. 6.4.2).** We trained ST-NDT, a contrastive Transformer and the top-ranked co-smoothing entry on the NLB MC_Maze leaderboard, on the same macaque windows (12-run sweep, same selection rule as the other baselines). NLDisco keeps the highest Spec and Sel for recent deceleration and much higher coverage for fast leftward target reach (0.991 vs 0.460), where ST-NDT's latent has the highest Spec and Sel.
2. ★ **Multi-session NLDisco (Sec. 3, 4.3, App. 6.4.6).** NLDisco can now be trained across sessions with shared latents plus session-specific ones. With the paper's session-A model frozen, we fit only a new input mapping and decoder on three further recording days, using neural data alone. The wheel-activity latent keeps its interpretation in each new session (AUROC 0.89-0.92; the best of all 192 shared latents every time), as do the three appendix latents. A model trained jointly on four sessions has a single wheel latent shared by all four (AUROC 0.90-0.92).
4. ★ **Environmental features (App. 6.4.10).** On Allen Visual Coding Neuropixels data, latents of a model trained without stimulus labels are tuned to Gabor position, grating temporal frequency, a specific natural image and a movie segment (held-out AUROC 0.77-0.97).
5. ★ **Batch-size sensitivity (App. 6.4.7).** With k fixed and B = 512, 1024 and 2048 (three seeds each), every reported feature is recovered; AUROC shifts by at most 0.033, within seed-to-seed variation.
6. ★ **Held-out validation of interpretations (App. 6.4.8).** Retraining on 80% of each recording and choosing each feature's latent on that part alone, all eight reported features keep their Sel and AUROC on the held-out 20% (mean change at most 0.02).
7. ★ **Feature-discovery workflow (App. 6.3.5).** We describe how dashboards and AI agents are used to cycle through latents and refine candidate features, with example dashboards. For the mouse dataset, one researcher made a first pass over 100 latents in under an hour.
8. **Dictionary-size sensitivity (App. 6.4.9).** From D = 128 to 1024 at fixed k, 88-92% of the D = 192 model's latents reappear and each reported feature's best latent keeps AUROC 0.77-0.99, while dead latents increase (mouse data: 0, about 50 and about 257 at D = 192, 512 and 1024).
9. **Activation-threshold sensitivity (App. 6.4.3).** Re-scoring the baseline latents across activation rates, Sel changes by at most 0.044 between half and twice the original rate for the three rarer features. At equal coverage NLDisco's latents have the highest Sel for all four features, and AUROC does not depend on the threshold.
10. **Reconstruction comparison (App. 6.4.4, Table S5).** With an identical Poisson readout from each method's representation to held-out spike counts, NLDisco matches LangevinFlow and exceeds CEBRA-Time (0.156, 0.159 and 0.115 bits/spike), ST-NDT is highest (0.253), and NLDisco's own decoder is the best of the models' own decoders (0.155 vs 0.148 and 0.141).
11. ★ **Related work (Sec. 4.2, App. 6.2).** We added switching dynamical systems, PSID, DFINE, pi-VAE and POYO, and how their targets (dynamical regimes, latent manifolds, multi-session decoding) relate to NLDisco's individual sparse features.
12. ★ **CEBRA and NLB (Sec. 4.2).** Corrected: NLB lists only the supervised CEBRA-Behavior. We use the label-free CEBRA-Time and now also ST-NDT.
13. ★ **Figure 3.** The dashed curves are now in the figure legend.

Together, these results show the practical utility you asked for: interpretations learned without behavioral supervision that hold on held-out data, across recording days, and for environmental as well as behavioral variables. We hope they address your concerns and would be glad to clarify anything further.

## Optional item 3, if the discovery comparison is included as measured

3. **End-to-end discovery comparison (appendix).** On a new mouse session with a discovery/held-out split, we compared NLDisco, CEBRA-Time, LangevinFlow, ST-NDT and PCA under one protocol fixed in advance: 23 behavior features specified before any model was inspected, and three independent raters inspecting the same 32 random latents per method with identical criteria and held-out validation (Fleiss' kappa 0.94). NLDisco recovers 10 of the 23 prespecified features (ST-NDT 12, PCA 9, CEBRA-Time 5, LangevinFlow 2), and its validated latents are the most selective (median held-out Sel 0.98 vs 0.83-0.92). ST-NDT has the largest share of latents with a validated interpretation (90%; LangevinFlow 63%, CEBRA-Time 29%, NLDisco 8%, PCA 6%).
